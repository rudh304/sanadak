About the idea

Startups and sole establishments are widespread, but they often have no IT department of their own. When a network, software, or hardware problem hits, their work stops. Sanadak Tech is a platform where a customer reports the problem and the system automatically routes it to the right technician. The technician accepts the request, then handles it remotely or on site until it is resolved.

  • Networks
  • Software
  • Hardware

The request journey

  1. Customer submits a ticket
  2. System classifies and routes
  3. Technician accepts
  4. Work and resolution
  5. Ticket closure and service rating

Project name
Sanadak Tech
Main objective
Build an online platform that lets startups and sole establishments report their technical problems. The platform automatically routes each ticket to the right technician and tracks it through to resolution, reducing business downtime and its cost.
Project description and products
  • Customer portal (web and mobile): submit a ticket, classify it (networks and infrastructure / software / hardware), attach images, track status, rate the service.
  • Smart routing engine: assigns tickets by specialty, location, and technician availability.
  • Technician app (mobile): accept or decline requests, update status live, close tickets.
  • Admin dashboard: manage customers and technicians, reports and performance indicators.
  • Online payment and notification integration (SMS / Push / email).

Item 2

Project Scope and WBS

Scope statement

In scope

Requirements analysis, system and interface design, development (customer portal, technician app, admin dashboard, routing engine), payment and notification integration, cloud hosting, testing, training the operations team and technicians, and a pilot launch in the city of Jeddah.

Out of scope

Running the service after launch, expanding to other cities, hiring technicians, supplying spare parts and hardware, marketing campaigns.

Key deliverables

Requirements document, interface designs, the platform with its four components, test reports, user guides, project closure report.

Assumptions

Contracted technicians are available before launch, and core requirements remain stable after approval.

Constraints

Budget of SAR 500,000, duration of 32 weeks, compliance with the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law.

    How it is calculated

    In the Forward Pass we start from the first activity: an activity's early start (ES) is the largest EF among its predecessors, and EF = ES + duration. In the Backward Pass we start from the end of the project: the late finish (LF) is the smallest LS among its successors, and LS = LF − duration. Then Float = LS − ES. Activities with zero float form the critical path, and any delay in them delays the whole project. For example, activity H starts at 11 and can start as late as 14, so its float is 3 weeks.

    How the budget is built

    Estimating method

    We used bottom-up estimating: we estimated each WBS work package separately, then added them up to reach the total activity cost. We then added two kinds of reserve. The contingency reserve covers known risks, is controlled by the project manager, and is part of the cost baseline. The management reserve covers unknown risks, is controlled by the sponsor, and sits outside the baseline but inside the total budget.

    Response strategies for negative risks

    Avoid

    Change the plan to remove the risk entirely. Example, R1: freeze requirements after approval.

    Transfer

    Shift the risk's impact to a third party. Example, R5: a service level agreement with the hosting provider.

    Mitigate

    Reduce the risk's probability or impact. Example, R2: assign the strongest developers and review weekly.

    Accept

    Acknowledge the risk and deal with it if it occurs. Example, R7: continuous documentation and knowledge sharing.
